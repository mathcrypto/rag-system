Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems work best when documents are split into
coherent chunks before embedding. Chunk size should be measured in tokens, not characters,
because embedding models like Voyage process tokens. A common production default is 512
tokens with about 64 tokens of overlap so context is preserved across chunk boundaries.

Overlap helps when an answer spans two adjacent sections. Without overlap, retrieval can
miss important sentences that sit near a split. Metadata such as source path, filename,
mime type, page number, and chunk index makes debugging and citations much easier later.

When building a production pipeline, keep raw files separate from processed chunks. If you
change the chunking strategy, re-process from raw data without re-downloading everything.
Evaluate retrieval quality with hit rate, MRR, and NDCG before tuning the generator prompt.
